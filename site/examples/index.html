One loop at three different settings

All three videos are the same 32-frame bouncing ball over the same four-to-the-floor bar at 120 BPM, hitting the ground on the same two frames. Only the setting named under each one is different. Turn the sound on.

What to listen for

All three are locked to the beat. The ball is on the ground on the downbeat in every one and stays there for the whole sixteen seconds instead of sliding out. What differs is everything else about the movement.

Evenly spread, the animation is a metronome. Correct, and a little lifeless. Eased, the frames either side of the beat go past in a few milliseconds each while the middle ones are held far longer, and the bounce reads as an impact rather than a position. Same thirty-two frames in both.

Half time is the setting people reach for last and want first. A GIF drawn to loop once a second does not have to hit every beat of a 120 BPM track. Letting it cover two beats often looks better than the literal reading.
