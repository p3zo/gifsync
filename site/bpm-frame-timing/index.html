BPM to frame timing

How long one beat lasts at a given tempo, and how long to hold each frame so a whole number of frames fills it exactly.

Why this is not simply beat length divided by frames

It is, if you are writing the timing into a video. It is not, if you are writing it back into a GIF. The GIF format stores a delay per frame in hundredths of a second, so the only durations it can express are 10ms, 20ms, 30ms and so on. At 120 BPM over eight frames a beat wants 62.5ms a frame, and the nearest a GIF can hold is 60ms. That loses 20ms on every beat, about a third of a frame, and 2.4 seconds over a minute.

Short delays are worse. Most browsers refuse a delay of one hundredth of a second and substitute a tenth, so a fast loop written as 10ms a frame plays at a tenth of the speed you asked for.

This is why Gifsync writes an MP4. Frame timestamps in an MP4 are written to the microsecond, so the beats land where they were put.

A frame held for less than one refresh of the display, about 16.7ms on a 60Hz screen, will not be seen however exactly it is timed. Nothing in the table is quite that short, but 16 frames at 180 BPM comes close.

Going the other way: beats from a frame count

If the GIF is fixed and the question is how many beats its loop should cover, divide the frame count by the beats you want and check the answer is a whole number. Twenty frames over eight beats gives 2.5, so some beats hold three frames and others two, a 50% swing in speed that is quite visible. Twenty frames over ten beats gives two, even all the way round. Pick a beat count that divides the frame count.
